\subsection{MLP}

Started with a baseline model with hidden\_size = 256, dropout = 0.2, optimizer = SGD, and learning\_rate = 0.01. Used a batch of 64 and 10 epochs. Here, we varied the activation layers. Tried out ReLU, LeakyReLU, and GELU. Using a 90/10 split for training and validation, LeakyReLU resulted in the highest validation accuracy \= 0.9018. 

Will continue varying the optimizers, using LeakyReLU as the activation function. Tried out SGD, Adam, and AdamW. Again using a 90/10 split for training and validation, AdamW resulted in the highest validation accuracy \= 0.8650.

Tried out learning rate [0.001, 0.01, 0.1]. Learning rate \= 0.01 resulted in the highest validation accuracy of 0.9025. 

Tried out hidden sizes of 128, 256, and 512. Hidden size of 128 was the best, with a validation accuracy of 0.8722.

Tried out dropout values of 0.0, 0.2, and 0.5. Dropout \= 0.0 resulted in the highest validation accuracy of 0.8788.

\subsection{CNN}
We started by setting seeds for reproducibility, and then importing the MNIST dataset from \texttt{torchvision.datasets}. After importing the train and test separately, we split the training dataset into two subsets: what we will use for training (90\%), and validation (10\%). Setting batch size to 64, we created data loaders that will then load the data into the models.

We set up a basic convolutional neural network, similar to the one depicted in Figure \ref{fig:cnn}, with two convolutional layers and max pooling between, with a customisable activation function.

Defining functions for training, validating, and testing, we then moved to the systematic testing of parameters.

We tested different activation functions:
\begin{description}
	\item[ReLU]
		\[
		f(x) =
		\begin{cases}
			x & x \ge 0 \\
			0 & \text{otherwise}
		\end{cases}
		.\] 
	\item[Leaky ReLU]
		\[
		f(x) =
		\begin{cases}
			x & x \ge 0 \\
			mx & \text{otherwise}
		\end{cases}
		.\] 
	\item[GeLU]
		\[
		f(x) = x \cdot\Phi(x)
		.\] 
\end{description}
